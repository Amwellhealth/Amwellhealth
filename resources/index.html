Muslim Mental Health Organizations

Khalil Center

A Muslim-focused psychotherapy and spiritual wellness organization combining clinical psychology with Islamic traditions. Founded by Dr. Hooman Keshavarzi, the organization provides mental health services through multiple locations and virtual care options.

Visit website

Olive Branch Wellness — Chicago

A Chicago-based mental health practice emphasizing culturally responsive therapy and a decolonizing approach to mental health care.

Maristan — California

A Muslim mental health nonprofit associated with Dr. Rania Awaad and grounded in Islamic traditions. Its services include mental health care and educational initiatives informed by Islamic psychology.

Read more

Muslim Community Center for Human Services — Texas

An organization providing mental health and counseling services to underserved communities. Its work includes support for individuals and families experiencing psychological and social difficulties.

Visit website

Mental Health Helplines

Naseeha

A Muslim-focused mental health helpline offering confidential, faith-sensitive emotional support.

866-NASEEHA

Visit website

Amala Youth Hopeline

A peer-support resource for Muslim youth seeking emotional support and understanding.

855-95-AMALA

Nisa Helpline

A support service offering peer counseling, referrals, and resources for Muslim women across North America.

Advocacy, Education, and Therapist Directories

Institute for Muslim Mental Health

Provides educational resources, community outreach, and information that helps connect individuals with Muslim mental health professionals.

Muslim Wellness Foundation

Works to reduce stigma surrounding mental illness, addiction, and trauma among American Muslims through education, training, and community dialogue.

SEEMA Mental Health

Provides mental health advocacy, support groups, and resources for individuals and families affected by mental health conditions.

American Muslim Health Professionals (AMHP)

Supports health education, mental health advocacy, professional development, and initiatives addressing underserved communities.

ISNA Mental Health Initiative

Supports efforts to improve mental health awareness, collaboration, and access to relevant resources within Muslim communities.

Institute for Social Policy and Understanding (ISPU)

Conducts research on American Muslim communities, including social experiences, well-being, and mental health-related issues.

Stanford Muslim Mental Health & Islamic Psychology Lab

A research initiative focused on advancing culturally and religiously informed approaches to Muslim mental health and Islamic psychology.

These external resources are provided for informational purposes only and do not constitute endorsements by AMWELL Research Lab. They are not a substitute for professional mental health care. Helplines listed here are peer-support and emotional-support services, not emergency services — in an emergency, call or text 988 or call 911.
